## Clusters and viewing questions

1. **Baseline visual calibration (#1–4).** Smooth cubics, Clebsch cubic, and a conic point. Record camera, bounded sphere, and light positions before interpreting a topological event.
2. **Exceptional `E₆` (#5–8, #28).** One `E₆` singularity rotates, then appears as the special member in families whose nearby members have `2A₂`, `D₅`, `D₄`, or `A₅` singularities. Compare the *number and type of singular points* in each nearby member.
3. **Whitney umbrella (#9–12).** A nonisolated singular locus deforms to smooth, `2A₁`, or `A₃` configurations. This is a necessary counterexample to the idea that every visually sharp feature is an isolated normal Du Val point.
4. **Three planes and the Cayley cubic (#13–16).** The Cayley cubic has **four `A₁` nodes**. Its deformation is a useful multi-node demonstration but should not be mistaken for the resolution of one node.
5. **Isolated and multiple `A₂` points (#17–22).** Compare a solitary `A₂` point over conjugate planes with three real `A₂` points; nearby fibres smooth or split to `A₁` points.
6. **Branching `D₄` (#23–25).** A `D₄` point deforms to three `A₁` points or a smooth cubic. This is a very good visual counterpart to a *separate* `D₄` resolution movie, in which four exceptional curves form a trivalent Dynkin graph.
7. **`A₅` and adjacency (#26–32).** Especially #31, `A₅ → A₄`, already singled out in this project's earlier notes. Compare it with #30, `A₅ → 2A₂`, and #28, `E₆ → A₅`.

### High-value direct comparisons for `resolution`

- [#5 — rotating `E₆`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B0jwiY6TKf0) and [#8 — `E₆ → D₄`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3lODh1vmgSQ): distinguish stable geometry from changing coefficients.
- [#12 — Whitney umbrella `→ A₃`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1mZa2NVoDKk): note that a line of singularities can disappear or change type.
- [#15 — rotating Cayley cubic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lDBiizaBUJM) and [#16 — its smoothing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6qkoHHZ8kvY): four globally distinct ordinary double points versus their nearby smooth member.
- [#24 — `D₄ → 3A₁`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ilz5J8Xuydc): a trivalent *resolution graph* is **not** the same as the three isolated singularities of a generic deformed cubic.
- [#31 — `A₅ → A₄`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VDaEZpKbYqQ): compare chains of 5 and 4 exceptional curves *on separately resolved fibres*, not inside Madore's film.

## Author's actual rendering protocol

The primary project page states:

- Computed using **POV-Ray** on **Debian GNU/Linux**.
- Each original is a **12-second seamless loop**, MPEG-2, PAL 720×576 at 25 fps.
- Same **fixed clipping sphere** in every film, so unbounded cubic surfaces have a smooth bounded outer edge.
- **Fixed green light above, red above/right, blue below**; the *surface itself* is neutral.
- In the phrase “deformation of **foo** to **bar**,” the generic part of the loop is **bar** and the special fibre **foo** appears at **3 seconds** (and again around **9 seconds** in the looped YouTube description for several examples). This is a convention about **specialization in a family**, not the temporal sequence of resolution.
- The author placed **the entire DVD, its source, and related material in the public domain**, while explicitly requesting acknowledgement.

**Metadata discrepancy worth retaining:** the YouTube description for video **#15** says “four A4 singularities,” but **its own title and the author's original DVD index specify four A1 singularities**. For the Cayley cubic, `4A₁` is correct; do not propagate the description's typo.

## What this does NOT depict

**Deformation** varies coefficients of `F_t(x,y,z)=0`; **resolution** constructs a proper birational morphism `π:Y→X` and replaces the singular point by exceptional rational curves whose intersection matrix can be recorded. The movies give excellent real-algebraic shape/lighting/timing references, **not the missing birational map**.

The cubic-surface `E₆` examples do not substitute for a full complex-surface `E₈` resolution. A complex surface is real four-dimensional; a plot of `F(x,y,z)=0` in real 3-space is only its real locus. The intended project must label any slice, projection, Milnor fibre, link, or schematic dual graph honestly.
