The lecture starts from finite subgroup quotients of `C²`, reviews the ADE equations, and carries out point blowups of the `E₈` surface singularity. Borcherds emphasizes that an individual blowup may make only a subtle improvement and that one must examine all projective coordinate charts.

## Quotients and the ADE list

The cyclic subgroup of order `n` acts on `C[u,v]` by

```text
u ↦ ζu,    v ↦ ζ⁻¹v,    ζⁿ=1.
```

Its invariant ring is generated by

```text
X=uⁿ,    Y=vⁿ,    Z=uv,       XY=Zⁿ.
```

After a linear change of complex coordinates, this is of type `A_(n−1)`. The other finite subgroups of `SL₂(C)` lead to the `D` and `E` cases, with binary tetrahedral, octahedral, and icosahedral groups corresponding to `E₆`, `E₇`, `E₈`.

| type | normal form over C |
| --- | --- |
| `A_n` | `x²+y²+zⁿ⁺¹=0` |
| `D_n` | `x²+y²z+zⁿ⁻¹=0` |
| `E₆` | `x²+y³+z⁴=0` |
| `E₇` | `x²+y³+yz³=0` |
| `E₈` | `x²+y³+z⁵=0` |

These equations describe **complex** surfaces. Their ordinary real loci are useful projected/sliced visual references, but do not show the full geometry of a complex resolution.

## Reconstructing the eight ordinary point blowups of E₈

Start with the isolated surface singularity

```text
F₀(x,y,z) = x² + y³ + z⁵ = 0.
```

For a blowup at the origin, introduce homogeneous directions `[X:Y:Z]∈P²` and use one of three affine charts. In each chart, divide the substituted equation by the appropriate maximal common power of the exceptional coordinate to obtain the **strict transform**. That division is essential; drawing the total transform would introduce a misleading extraneous component.

### Blowup 1 — z chart

Set `x=z a, y=z b`. Then

```text
F₀ = z²(a² + z b³ + z³).
F₁(a,b,z) = a² + z b³ + z³.
```

The strict transform has a residual singular point at `a=b=z=0`. The `x` and `y` charts have smooth strict transforms (for example the `y` chart gives `a²+y+y³c⁵=0`).

### Blowup 2 — b chart at that singular point

From `F₁`, set `a=b c, z=b d`. Dividing by `b²` gives

```text
F₂(c,b,d) = c² + b²d + bd³ = 0.
```

The origin is again singular. The other two charts of this blowup are smooth.

The visible polynomial has not monotonically decreased in complexity: a chart-by-chart ledger matters more than a naive term-count metric.

### Blowup 3 — two distinct residual singularities

Blow up the origin of `F₂`.

**b chart:** set `c=b e, d=b f`, so

```text
F₃A(e,b,f) = e² + bf + b²f³ = 0.
```

At the origin the nondegenerate quadratic part is `e²+bf`, so this is an **A₁ ordinary double point**.

**d chart:** set `c=d e, b=d g`, so

```text
F₃B(e,d,g) = e² + dg² + d²g
           = e² + dg(g+d) = 0.
```

The binary cubic `dg(g+d)` has three distinct linear factors, giving a **D₄ singularity**. This singular point is distinct from the A₁ point: they occupy different locations in the newly introduced projective directions. The `c` chart is smooth.

The third blowup therefore exposes a useful branching event:

```text
E₈
 └─ blowup 1: one residual singular point
     └─ blowup 2: one residual singular point
         └─ blowup 3: two singular points
             ├─ A₁: one more blowup resolves it (blowup 4)
             └─ D₄: blow it up (blowup 5)
                 ├─ A₁: blowup 6
                 ├─ A₁: blowup 7
                 └─ A₁: blowup 8
```

**Important:** this is a *dependency tree of singular points and operations*, **not** the exceptional-curve intersection graph. Showing it as if it were the E₈ Dynkin diagram would be incorrect.

### Blowup 4 — resolve the first A₁

An ordinary blowup resolves `F₃A` at its ordinary double point. The D₄ branch remains to be treated separately.

### Blowup 5 — the D₄ point produces three A₁ points

For `F₃B=e²+dg(g+d)`:

- In the `d` chart set `e=d p, g=d q`; its strict transform is `p²+dq(q+1)=0`, singular at `(p,d,q)=(0,0,0)` and `(0,0,-1)`.
- In the `g` chart set `e=g p, d=g q`; its strict transform is `p²+gq(q+1)=0`, singular at `q=0` and `q=-1` on its exceptional line.
- The two charts overlap at one of those points, so there are **three distinct A₁ points**, not four.

Each point has a nondegenerate quadratic tangent cone after a suitable local translation.

### Blowups 6–8 — resolve the three A₁ points

Blow up each of the three ordinary double points once. The resulting surface is smooth. The ordinary point-blowup sequence has length

```text
3 preliminary blowups + 1 A₁ + 1 D₄ + 3 A₁ = 8.
```

## Exceptional curves are a different graph

The minimal resolution has eight irreducible exceptional rational curves `E₁,…,E₈`, with

```text
Eᵢ² = −2
Eᵢ·Eⱼ = 1 when the corresponding E₈ Dynkin vertices are adjacent
Eᵢ·Eⱼ = 0 otherwise.
```

The intersection matrix is `−C_E8`. One must **compute** the curve incidence through the charts; it cannot be inferred simply by copying the branching tree of still-singular points above. An intermediate exceptional divisor may also have multiplicity or singularities; it should not automatically be drawn as a finished smooth sphere.

## What this gives the animation project

Borcherds supplies the mathematical backbone absent from a deformation film:

1. **Before:** show the original `x²+y³+z⁵=0` with a fixed real slice and record that the underlying complex surface has real dimension four.
2. **Chart transition:** show the current singular point, projective direction, substitution, removed exceptional factor, and new strict-transform equation.
3. **First branching scene:** blowup 3 genuinely leaves an `A₁` and a `D₄` in different charts. Two linked camera views should identify those two *distinct projective points*.
4. **D₄ scene:** its cubic `dg(g+d)` has three different tangent directions. Projective charts show the three resulting A₁ centers. This is particularly suitable for a three-dimensional *schematic* camera animation, not a literal 3D embedding of the complex resolution.
5. **End:** display the eight exceptional `P¹` components and their **separately verified** E₈ intersection tree; offer the reverse contraction to the singular point.

Show two **noninterchangeable graphs** simultaneously: the process tree of remaining singular points and the intersection tree of exceptional curves. They encode different information.
